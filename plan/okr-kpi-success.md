As a product manager, measuring success requires looking beyond simple "Daily Active Users." Since this tool deals with high-stakes, emotionally charged processes involving money and health, your metrics must reflect **trust**, **efficacy**, and **workflow completion**.

Here is a breakdown of OKRs (Objectives/Key Results) and KPIs (Key Performance Indicators), categorized by the aspect of the business or user experience they measure.

---

### 🥇 Focus Area 1: User Value & Efficacy (The "Does it work?" metrics)
These are the most important measurements because they validate your core value proposition: helping people recover money and fight confusion.

**Objective:** *Significantly reduce patient financial uncertainty by enabling successful appeals and clarity.*

**Key Results (KR):** (Measurable targets for 3-6 months)
1.  Achieve a **15% increase** in the average dollar amount of recovered funds per active user within 90 days post-MVP launch. (The ultimate win.)
2.  Ensure that **40% of users** who initiate an appeal workflow successfully submit a completed, actionable packet to their insurer through our platform.
3.  Reduce the average time it takes a first-time user to log and categorize a denied claim from **(Baseline)** minutes down to **15 minutes**.

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):** (Daily/Weekly dashboard metrics)
*   **Appeal Success Rate:** (Number of successful appeals / Total number of claims submitted for appeal). This is your single most important KPI.
*   **Claim Documentation Completeness Score:** A score assigned to a claim file based on the presence and type of documents uploaded (e.g., 8/10: Policy + EOB + Denial Letter = High Score). Measures if the user followed process correctly.
*   **Confusion Rate:** Number of times a user revisits the same "Dictionary" term or workflow step within a single claim session, indicating continued confusion that needs clearer UX/guidance.

---

### 🥈 Focus Area 2: Product Health & Engagement (The "Is it sticky?" metrics)
These measure if users find enough value to come back and continue using the system.

**Objective:** *Establish the tool as the single, indispensable source of truth for all healthcare billing inquiries.*

**Key Results (KR):**
1.  Achieve a **retention rate of 30%** for users who initiate a claim in one month to submit documentation on a second claim within the next two months. (Indicates deep trust and reliance).
2.  Increase the average number of *related* KBs/Knowledge Bases viewed per user session by **50%**. (Measures feature discovery and cross-referencing use).

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):**
*   **Workflow Abandonment Rate:** The percentage of users who start a workflow (e.g., Denial Analyzer) but do not proceed to the final action step (Generating the Roadmap or Template). *High abandonment here signals UX failure.*
*   **Feature Adoption Waterfall:** Track how many users move from using Feature A $\rightarrow$ to using Feature B. This helps you see the natural progression of a user's needs and prioritize future integration points.

---

### 🥉 Focus Area 3: Operational & Business (The "Is it sustainable?" metrics)
These measure the efficiency and viability of running the product.

**Objective:** *Build a scalable, reliable, and legally defensible platform.*

**Key Results (KR):**
1.  Reduce customer support tickets related to basic process questions by **25%** within 6 months (because the tool answered it).
2.  Achieve a legal/security compliance audit passing grade with zero critical findings in Year 1.

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):**
*   **Mean Time To Success:** The average time (in days) between the user uploading the initial denial and logging an "Action Completed" milestone on the roadmap. A decreasing MTTS indicates your product is accelerating their resolution process.
*   **Support Ticket Volume by Feature Area:** Tracks which specific features are causing confusion, directly guiding where you need to add better in-app help or rewrite documentation.

---

### 🌟 Product Manager's Quick Takeaway: Prioritization Focus

If you can only track three things initially, focus on these:

1.  **Appeal Success Rate (Efficacy):** Does it help people win money/coverage?
2.  **Workflow Abandonment Rate (Product Health):** Are users quitting because the process is too hard or confusing?
3.  **Time-to-Completion for a Claim (Operational Efficiency):** Is your tool making this huge task *feel* and *actually* feel easier than existing methods?
